High chance ≠ high Lift
A Sure Thing buys either way. Their Lift is about zero, so a discount only pays them for what they’d do anyway.
How an offer is scored
Lift × margin − expected cost. Send the best positive one. Nothing positive? No Offer.
Personalised Offer Engine · 06 · Our average Customer: 3% chance to buy in a week; a full discount adds about 2 points
No peeking at the answer key
How the engine estimates Lift
Step 1
Group alike
Same loyalty tier, same time since last order, coupon user or not.
Step 2
Compare
Who bought within 7 days of a random past offer, vs. a similar moment with none.
Step 3
Take the gap
That gap is the Estimated Lift. Too little data? Use the Offer Type’s average.
Then we grade it against true Lift, decile by decile.

The engine
How a Decision is made
1
Trigger
Something happens to a Customer.
2
Rules
Build the Eligible Set: which offers are allowed, and how deep.
3
Claude
Picks one offer or No Offer, sets its terms, writes the message.
4
Validation
Re-checks every Rule and the caption.
5
Offer Card
Goes out on Telegram, or No Offer.
What starts it
Abandoned cart, points expiring, 45 quiet days, first delivery, the Daily Sweep at 10:00, or /offers.
If Claude fails
A broken Rule, an API error or a 20-second timeout: the rules chooser decides instead. That’s the Fallback.
Everything logged
Who chose, why, the Binding Rule and the rule version, stage by stage.
Personalised Offer Engine · 08 · Claude decides for Customers on a Telegram chat; the rules chooser for everyone else
Marketing stays in charge
Rules in plain English
Never discount below 5% margin
Send at most 2 offers per 7 days
Give gold Customers at most 25% off
Spend at most ₹50,000 a week
Keep 10% of Customers as a Holdout
Pause after 3 “Not now”s in 14 days
Strictest wins
When Rules clash, the tightest one decides. It’s saved on the Decision as the Binding Rule.
Stop means stop
Opt-out beats everything until the Customer sends /start again.
Change it safely
Preview the impact before saving. Every version is kept, with who and why. One-click revert, emergency withdraw.
